# Playbook: tutorial and how-to (tutorial.v4)

You chose this playbook because the footage teaches something: a recipe, a repair, a technique, a setup. Target 25 to 45 seconds.

**The format.** The format you chose in plan_edit (from list_formats) sets the structure: its hook, its middle, its payoff and its text pattern, timed against the song's drop. This playbook is the craft of the genre inside that structure. Where they differ on structure (how long the hook runs, where the payoff lands, which text the video has), the format wins; on craft (the cuts, the rhythm, the movement, the sound), this playbook does. The creator's style and settings still decide the look.

**Hook (0 to 2 s).** The result first: "here is what you will make". Open on the finished thing, or on the most satisfying moment of the process, for 1 to 2 seconds, with a title that names the result, on screen by 0.5 s.

**Arc.** The result, then the steps in order, then the result again. Each step shows its key action clearly, once. Trim or speed up the slow and repetitive parts (kneading, stirring, waiting, sanding): set_speed 2 to 4, or cut them. Keep the one moment per step that the viewer must see at normal speed.

**Text.** A numbered step label on each step (label_box, short and uppercase, such as "1 CRACK THE EGGS"), arriving with the step and staying 1.5 to 3 seconds, in the upper third or the centre, never over the hands or the work. At most one text on screen at a time.

**Cutting.** Steps are 2 to 5 seconds each, and they vary; a quick action is a short shot. Cut on the action (the hand reaching, the tool touching the work), not before it. Do not skip a step the next step depends on.

**Energy.** Plain: plain cuts between steps (a crossfade only to show that time has passed), a punch_in on a detail the viewer must see. No effect transitions unless the creator's style has them.

**Sound.** A clip where the person explains out loud is full (set_clip_audio mode full); the others are a low bed (mode bed), or muted when the room is noisy.

**Ending.** The finished result held for a moment, with a short line ("DONE") or a call to action. It can loop back to the first frame.
