# Playbook: workout and fitness (workout.v8)

You chose this playbook because the footage is training: lifts, sets, a pose, a transformation. Target 15 to 35 seconds.

**The format.** The format you chose in plan_edit (from list_formats) sets the structure: its hook, its middle, its payoff and its text pattern, timed against the song's drop. This playbook is the craft of the genre inside that structure. Where they differ on structure (how long the hook runs, where the payoff lands, which text the video has), the format wins; on craft (the cuts, the rhythm, the movement, the sound), this playbook does. The creator's style and settings still decide the look.

**Hook (0 to the drop).** The most striking frame first: a peak flex, the heaviest lockout, the best rep, the back pose. With a song the hook runs until its drop (2 to 4.5 s in) and the first cut lands on the drop, a plain cut on the hit with a punch-in on it: the reveal. Without one the first shot is 1 to 2 seconds long. A big title (in the creator's style when there is one; else title_bold or title_outline, 2 to 4 words{{if .Has "op:add_graphic"}}; at lively or hype a title_pop whose words land on the first beats{{end}}) pops in by 0.5 s, in the top band above the head, and leaves before the drop. It stays in the hook: when the head is high, a short title stacks beside it, so keep its words few and short. Nothing sits still before the drop: the hook shot has the living camera or a punch_in on the first beat.

**Rhythm.**
- Every cut sits on the beat grid (the tempo in your plan; 120 to 128 BPM when no sound is chosen).
- The pace targets from plan_edit (the creator's measured shot lengths scaled by the vibe, or the defaults) set how long the shots run: aim the median shot at the target, keep every shot between the hook and the ending under the longest, and hold the ending no longer than the target ending.
- Vary the length: 1 to 2 beats for hype moments, and a full rep only where the pace allows a shot that long. Slow lifts are the usual problem (a rep of 4 to 5 s is longer than most creators' shots), and three tools fit them to the pace: cut at the rep's turning point (turn_ms in find_moments: half a rep, from its start to the turn or from the turn to its end, on a beat), switch at the turning point to another angle of the same exercise (front and behind), or play the rep faster with set_speed or a speed ramp. Use them when the pace target needs them, not as a habit. Never put more than two shots of the same length in a row.
- The cuts speed up toward the end.

**Movement and continuity.**
- Cut at the top or the bottom of the movement, never in the middle: a shot starts and ends on a rep's start_ms, its end_ms or its turn_ms (check_edit accepts those within what the analysis can measure, about 150 ms, and a little more on a slow rep, which lingers at its turns).
- Cut on motion where you can (the bar going up in one shot, the next shot starting on the same movement). Skip dead time completely.
- Prefer the cleanest, sharpest reps (score 0.7 and above). Keep the exposure even from shot to shot: the footage is often low-key (a lit lifter in a dark gym), which is fine, so do not try to brighten it, but do not alternate a very dark angle with a much lighter one.

**Angles.** A set is not one 10 second tripod take. With two angles of an exercise (front and behind, close and wide), switch between them at rep boundaries on a beat, as often as the pace target asks (the turning point of a rep in one angle to the same phase in the other is a clean switch); with one angle, keep it running with the living camera and a punch-in on a strong beat.

**Energy (by the vibe).** Plain cuts on the beat at every vibe: effect transitions (punch, zoom_through, whip, flash) read as an automatic AI edit to creators, so use them only when the creator's settings ask for them, and then only the kinds named there. Every shot has the living camera (set_motion handheld with no id), and the shot that holds the drop or a strong beat a handheld punch-in at_ms there. At lively: cuts on the strong beats and the phrase starts, a punch-in on the drop and on the biggest moment, text that pops in. At hype: shots of 1 to 2 beats after the hook, every cut on a beat or a hit, a punch-in on every phrase start. At calm: cuts on the strong beats only, shots that breathe. A speed ramp on the best rep at the most, and none for a creator of no effects.

**Text.** As the creator tells it (the settings: one title, story captions, dialogue or none; see "The text of the video" in the prompt). With story captions, a short line on every shot carries the story of the set (what changed, what they did, the result), in the creator's language and look, and there is no separate title. With one title (the default): a new title for this video, in the creator's template when there is one, never one of their old titles. A video of two or more exercises is a listicle: a numbered chapter title per exercise ("1. ROWS FOR THICKNESS", in the creator's title style and language) slides up with the exercise's first shot, in the top band above the head, and fades before the next. {{if .Has "op:add_graphic"}}At lively or hype the chapter titles are chapter_cards: the number on the exercise's first cut, the line on the next beat, about 2.5 seconds on screen; a counter can land the rep count of the best set on a beat. At calm keep plain text or a blur_in.{{end}} Never over the lifter's body. Word-highlight captions only when there is speech and the brief wants them.

**Sound.** With a song, the export is the song alone (no gym clatter under it): set_sound kind creator_audio with source_offset_ms so its drop lands at the end of the hook, and cut on its beats. Without one, the clips' own sound is the gym: set_clip_audio mode bed once with no id, so there is a low ambience under every shot (full only for a clip where someone coaches or talks), and the creator adds a trending sound in TikTok over the export; the beat grid you write is so the cuts will match it.
{{if .Has "sfx"}}Add generated sound effects with add_sfx on top of the bed, with restraint: a whoosh at the start of each transition the style allows, a sub_drop together with an impact on the drop, an impact on the hook's punch_in, a riser (tension) from the first frame when the hook's first cut is about 1.5 s in, so it peaks on that cut, and a sting on the last frame when the video loops. At most one effect sound per visual effect, and variant "auto" so repeated whooshes differ.
{{end}}{{if .Has "look:gym_moody"}}
**Look.** One grade for every shot, never a different one per shot, set once with set_look and no id after the cuts are final, and chosen from the clips' exposure notes. Gym footage is usually low-key (a lit lifter in a dark room), and a grade that darkens it crushes it to near black. So when the clips are noted low-key or underexposed, use no grade at all, or at most auto (a gentle curve that leaves the shadows alone). Use gym_moody (warm skin, a little contrast, a soft vignette; it leaves the shadows alone too) when the clips have no exposure note or are brighter. After a look, render_preview and check_edit: its crushed check measures the render against the footage, and if it fails, take the look off (set_look none) and say so in finish. Do not set exposure_ev or match yourself. When one clip is much brighter than the gym clips (a posing clip among them), call match_exposure: it sets bounded hints from the analysis that darken the brighter clip toward the rest.
{{end}}
**Ending.** Loop back to the hook: end on a held pose or the same frame the video opened on, so the video replays smoothly. Hold it no longer than the pace's ending; a longer hold needs a push and a punch-in on a beat (the living camera with its punch-in on the beat), or it reads as static.
